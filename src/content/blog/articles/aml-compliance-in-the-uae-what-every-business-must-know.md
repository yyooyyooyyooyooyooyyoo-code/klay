# AML Compliance in the UAE – What Every Business Must Know
Anti-money laundering (AML) compliance in the UAE is an important responsibility for businesses operating in regulated sectors. As the UAE continues to strengthen its financial system and international business environment, companies are expected to maintain appropriate controls to prevent money laundering, terrorist financing and the financing of prohibited activities.

Many business owners assume that AML regulations apply only to banks and financial institutions. However, the UAE framework also covers certain non-financial businesses and professions, virtual asset service providers and other regulated activities.

Depending on the nature of the business, these obligations may include customer identification, Know Your Customer (KYC) procedures, risk assessments, transaction monitoring, record keeping, internal policies and suspicious transaction reporting.

The UAE introduced a new federal AML framework through Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025, supported by Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025. These rules form an important part of the country's current approach to combating money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing.

For business owners, understanding AML compliance is not simply about avoiding penalties. It is about building a transparent, properly documented and sustainable business structure.

This guide explains who may need to comply with AML requirements in the UAE, what the main obligations involve and how businesses can establish an effective compliance framework.

## What Is AML Compliance in the UAE?
AML stands for Anti-Money Laundering. It refers to the laws, procedures and controls designed to prevent criminals from using legitimate businesses or financial systems to hide money obtained through illegal activities.

Money laundering generally involves concealing or disguising the origin, ownership or movement of illegally obtained funds so that they appear legitimate.

In the UAE, the AML framework also addresses:

- Combating the financing of terrorism (CFT).
- Countering the financing of proliferation (CPF).
- Identifying suspicious financial activity.
- Verifying customer and beneficial ownership information.
- Monitoring business relationships and transactions.
- Reporting suspicious activity to the competent authorities.
- Maintaining appropriate records and internal controls.
The current federal framework is based on Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 and its Executive Regulations under Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025.

These replaced the previous federal AML law and its earlier executive regulations. Businesses should therefore ensure that their compliance policies refer to the current legislation rather than relying only on older guidance.

## Who Needs to Follow AML Compliance Regulations in the UAE?
AML obligations do not apply identically to every company. The requirements depend on the business activity, regulatory classification, services offered and the relevant supervisory authority.

The main categories include financial institutions, Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs), and Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs).

### 1. Financial Institutions
Financial institutions are subject to AML requirements because they handle financial transactions, customer funds and other activities that may be exposed to financial crime risks.

Examples include:

- Banks.
- Exchange houses.
- Certain financial service providers.
- Insurance-related businesses covered by the applicable framework.
- Securities and investment-related firms.
- Other regulated financial institutions.
These businesses generally need structured customer verification, transaction monitoring, risk assessment, reporting and compliance governance systems.

### 2. Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs)
Certain non-financial businesses and professional activities also fall within the UAE AML framework.

The categories include:

- Auditors and accountants.
- Real estate agents and brokers.
- Dealers in precious metals and precious stones.
- Lawyers, notaries and other independent legal professionals when conducting specified transactions for clients.
- Company and trust service providers.
The exact obligations depend on the activity and the circumstances in which the business operates.

For example, a real estate broker involved in property transactions may have AML responsibilities that differ from those of a general retail business.

Similarly, a company service provider may need to apply customer due diligence when assisting with company formation, ownership or related services.

### 3. Virtual Asset Service Providers
Businesses providing regulated virtual asset services may be subject to additional AML and regulatory requirements.

Depending on their activity and location, these may include virtual asset exchanges, brokerage services, custody and other regulated services.

Virtual asset businesses should identify the appropriate regulator and comply with the applicable federal and local regulatory frameworks.

### Does Every UAE Company Need the Same AML Framework?
No. A company is not automatically subject to the same full AML programme simply because it is registered in the UAE.

A general trading company, a real estate brokerage, a bank and a virtual asset service provider have different risk profiles and regulatory obligations.

However, businesses should understand their customer base, payment flows, ownership structure and any AML obligations that arise from their actual activities.

A company should not assume that it is outside the AML framework without reviewing its activities and regulatory classification.

## Understanding the UAE AML Legal Framework in 2026
The UAE's current AML framework is based on federal legislation and supporting executive regulations, together with requirements issued by relevant supervisory authorities.

### Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025
Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 addresses anti-money laundering, combating the financing of terrorism and proliferation financing.

It establishes the principal federal legal framework for identifying, preventing and addressing financial crime.

The law sets out relevant responsibilities, offences, enforcement arrangements and obligations for entities covered by its provisions.

### Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025
Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025 provides the Executive Regulations supporting the federal AML law.

It sets out further requirements concerning risk assessment, customer due diligence, internal controls, record keeping, reporting and other compliance measures.

The resolution became effective on 14 December 2025.

### Regulatory and Supervisory Authorities
AML supervision is carried out by the relevant competent authorities according to the business activity and regulatory jurisdiction.

These may include:

- Central Bank of the UAE.
- Ministry of Economy.
- Securities and Commodities Authority or its successor regulatory framework, as applicable.
- Dubai Financial Services Authority (DFSA) for relevant DIFC-regulated activities.
- Financial Services Regulatory Authority (FSRA) for relevant ADGM-regulated activities.
- Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA) for activities within its jurisdiction.
- Other relevant federal and local supervisory authorities.
Businesses should identify their actual regulator rather than assuming that one authority supervises every UAE company.

## What Are the Main AML Compliance Requirements in the UAE?
Businesses subject to AML requirements must establish controls that reflect their actual money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing risks.

The following areas are central to an effective AML framework.

### 1. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)
Customer Due Diligence involves identifying and verifying customers before or during a business relationship, as required by the applicable rules.

A business may need to collect and verify:

- Customer's full legal name.
- Passport or other identification details.
- Emirates ID, where applicable.
- Residential or registered business address.
- Nature of the customer's business.
- Purpose of the business relationship.
- Expected transaction activity.
- Relevant ownership information.
For corporate customers, businesses may also need to understand the company's ownership structure and identify the individuals who ultimately own or control it.

CDD is not simply a one-time document collection exercise. Customer information should be reviewed and updated according to the customer's risk profile and applicable requirements.

### 2. Know Your Customer (KYC) Compliance
KYC is a key part of customer due diligence.

It helps businesses understand who they are dealing with, what the customer does and whether the relationship presents potential financial crime risks.

A practical KYC process may include:

- Verifying identity documents.
- Checking company registration information.
- Identifying beneficial owners.
- Understanding the source of funds where required.
- Reviewing the purpose of transactions.
- Screening against relevant sanctions lists.
- Identifying politically exposed persons where applicable.
The depth of KYC checks should be proportionate to the assessed risk.

### 3. Customer Risk Assessment
Businesses should assess the risk associated with customers, business relationships, products, services, delivery channels and geographic exposure.

A risk assessment may consider:

- Customer's country of residence or incorporation.
- Nature of the customer's business.
- Ownership complexity.
- Source of funds.
- Transaction size and frequency.
- Countries involved in payments.
- Use of intermediaries.
- Exposure to sanctions or politically exposed persons.
The results should help determine the appropriate level of customer due diligence and ongoing monitoring.

### 4. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)
Enhanced Due Diligence is applied when a customer, transaction or business relationship presents higher risk.

Depending on the circumstances, EDD may involve:

- Obtaining additional identification documents.
- Understanding the source of wealth and source of funds.
- Seeking additional information about the purpose of transactions.
- Obtaining appropriate senior management approval.
- Increasing the frequency of customer reviews.
- Applying closer transaction monitoring.
EDD should be based on the applicable legal requirements and the actual risk assessment.

### 5. Ongoing Transaction Monitoring
Businesses subject to AML requirements should monitor relevant transactions and business relationships.

The objective is to identify activity that does not match the customer's known profile, declared business activity or expected transaction pattern.

Potential warning signs may include:

- Unusual transaction values.
- Payments inconsistent with the customer's business.
- Unexpected third-party payments.
- Complex or unexplained transaction structures.
- Frequent transfers involving higher-risk jurisdictions.
- Unexplained changes in payment patterns.
- Transactions with no clear commercial purpose.
These indicators do not automatically prove criminal activity. They should be reviewed and escalated according to the business's internal procedures.

### 6. Suspicious Transaction Reporting
Where a business covered by the AML framework identifies suspicious activity, it may have a legal obligation to report it to the competent authority.

In the UAE, suspicious transaction reporting is generally made through the Financial Intelligence Unit's goAML system by entities required to use it.

A business should establish:

- An internal process for identifying suspicious activity.
- A clear escalation procedure.
- A designated compliance contact or officer where required.
- Appropriate reporting controls.
- Confidential handling of suspicious activity information.
- Records of relevant investigations and decisions.
Employees should not inform customers that a suspicious transaction report has been filed where disclosure is prohibited by law. This is commonly referred to as the prohibition on tipping off.

## What Is goAML Registration in the UAE?
goAML is the reporting system used by the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit to receive and process reports relating to suspicious transactions and other specified financial intelligence.

Entities that are required to report through the system must follow the applicable registration and reporting procedures.

Depending on the regulatory category, report types may include:

- Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs).
- Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs).
- Other reports required by the relevant rules and reporting guidance.
Businesses should not assume that every UAE company must register for goAML. The requirement depends on whether the entity falls within a category required to report through the system.

Where registration is required, businesses should ensure that the authorised users, compliance officer details and reporting procedures are properly established.

## Understanding Beneficial Ownership and UBO Requirements
Beneficial ownership is another important area of UAE corporate transparency.

A company's legal shareholder may not always be the individual who ultimately owns or controls it.

The Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) framework aims to identify the natural person or persons who ultimately own or control a legal entity.

Businesses may need to maintain accurate information about:

- Direct shareholders.
- Indirect ownership.
- Individuals exercising ultimate control.
- Relevant ownership percentages.
- Changes in ownership or control.
- Nominee arrangements, where applicable.
The UAE's beneficial ownership framework is separate from the customer due diligence process, although the two are closely connected.

Companies should review their UBO information and comply with the applicable filing, updating and record-maintenance requirements under the relevant company and regulatory rules.

## AML Compliance for Mainland and Free Zone Companies
Business owners often ask whether AML obligations differ between Mainland and Free Zone companies.

The answer depends on the activity and the applicable supervisory framework.

### Mainland Companies
Mainland companies may be subject to AML requirements where their activities fall within a regulated financial institution, DNFBP or another covered category.

For example, real estate brokerage, certain professional services and dealers in precious metals and stones may have specific obligations.

The relevant federal and local rules and the company's supervisory authority should be identified.

### Free Zone Companies
Free Zone companies are not automatically exempt from AML requirements.

A Free Zone entity may be subject to federal AML obligations and additional requirements imposed by its Free Zone authority or sector regulator.

Financial activities in specialised financial free zones may also be subject to rules issued by the relevant regulator.

### Offshore Companies
Offshore structures are not automatically outside AML scrutiny.

Their obligations depend on their legal form, activities, regulatory status, ownership and transactions.

Banks and other financial institutions may also conduct enhanced checks when dealing with companies that have complex ownership arrangements or cross-border transaction flows.

**The important principle:** AML compliance should be determined by the actual activity and regulatory classification, not simply by whether the company is Mainland, Free Zone or Offshore.

## Common AML Compliance Mistakes Businesses Should Avoid
Many businesses face compliance difficulties because AML requirements are considered only after the company has started operating.

The following mistakes can create unnecessary regulatory and banking risks.

### 1. Incorrect Business Activity Classification
Choosing a licence activity without understanding its regulatory implications may create problems later.

Businesses should ensure that their licence accurately reflects what they actually do.

### 2. Weak KYC Documentation
Collecting incomplete identification documents or failing to verify beneficial owners can create customer due diligence gaps.

### 3. No Formal Risk Assessment
Businesses should not rely on informal assumptions about customer or transaction risk.

A documented risk assessment helps establish why specific controls are appropriate.

### 4. Poor Record Keeping
Missing contracts, invoices, ownership records or customer verification documents can make it difficult to demonstrate compliance.

### 5. No Internal AML Policies
Where required, businesses should maintain documented procedures for customer acceptance, risk assessment, monitoring, escalation and reporting.

### 6. Ignoring Employee Training
Employees who interact with customers or process transactions should understand the relevant warning signs and escalation procedures.

### 7. Failure to Review Customer Information
Customer details and risk profiles can change. Businesses should update information in accordance with their risk-based review procedures and applicable legal requirements.

### 8. Assuming the Bank Handles All AML Responsibilities
Banks perform their own compliance checks, but this does not remove the legal obligations of a business that is itself subject to AML requirements.

### 9. Using Outdated AML Policies
Businesses should review their policies when legislation, regulatory guidance, business activities or risk exposure changes.

## What Happens If a Business Fails to Meet AML Requirements?
AML non-compliance can have serious consequences.

Depending on the violation, the applicable law and the competent authority's enforcement powers, consequences may include:

- Administrative financial penalties.
- Regulatory warnings or directions.
- Restrictions on business activities.
- Suspension or cancellation of relevant licences.
- Increased regulatory supervision.
- Criminal liability in serious cases.
- Reputational damage.
- Additional banking due diligence or account restrictions.
A bank may delay, restrict or terminate a business relationship based on its own risk assessment and applicable legal obligations. This is not an automatic consequence of every compliance gap, but weak documentation can create significant banking difficulties.

The exact penalty depends on the nature and seriousness of the breach. Businesses should consult the current legislation and their supervisory authority rather than relying on penalty amounts quoted in outdated articles.

## How to Build an Effective AML Compliance Framework
AML compliance should be integrated into business operations from the beginning.

A structured framework can help a company identify risks and establish appropriate controls.

### Step 1: Identify Your Regulatory Classification
Determine whether the company is a financial institution, DNFBP, VASP or another regulated entity.

Identify the relevant supervisory authority and the rules that apply.

### Step 2: Conduct a Business-Wide Risk Assessment
Review the company's activities, customer types, geographical exposure, products, services and transaction channels.

Document the risks and the controls needed to address them.

### Step 3: Establish AML Policies and Procedures
Prepare written policies covering:

- Customer onboarding.
- Customer due diligence.
- Beneficial ownership verification.
- Sanctions and relevant screening.
- Customer risk classification.
- Enhanced due diligence.
- Ongoing transaction monitoring.
- Suspicious activity escalation and reporting.
- Record keeping.
- Employee training.

### Step 4: Assign Compliance Responsibilities
Where required, appoint an appropriately qualified AML compliance officer or money laundering reporting officer.

Clearly define their responsibilities, authority and reporting arrangements.

### Step 5: Implement KYC and Customer Verification
Establish a consistent process for collecting, checking and updating customer information.

### Step 6: Establish Monitoring and Reporting Procedures
Ensure that unusual or suspicious activity can be identified, investigated and escalated.

Where required, use the goAML system to submit reports to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

### Step 7: Maintain Records
Keep appropriate records of customer due diligence, transactions, risk assessments, internal reviews, training and reporting decisions for the applicable retention period.

### Step 8: Train Employees
Provide training appropriate to employees' responsibilities and the risks associated with the company's activities.

### Step 9: Review the Framework Regularly
Update the AML programme when the company introduces new activities, enters new markets, changes ownership or faces new risk exposure.

## Why AML Compliance Matters During UAE Company Formation
AML considerations should be reviewed during company formation rather than after the business begins operating.

The selected business activity, ownership structure, customer base and expected payment flows may affect the company's regulatory obligations.

For example, a company providing general consulting services may have a different AML classification from a business acting as a company service provider or conducting regulated financial activities.

A company that plans to handle high-value goods, property transactions or virtual assets may also need to review additional requirements.

Before incorporating, business owners should consider:

- Whether the intended activity is regulated.
- Whether additional approvals or licences are required.
- Whether the business falls within a DNFBP or other AML category.
- Whether a compliance officer must be appointed.
- What customer verification procedures will be needed.
- How transactions and financial records will be maintained.
- Whether the selected jurisdiction and operating model are appropriate.
Choosing a licence based only on price or convenience may create problems if it does not accurately reflect the company's real activities.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Who needs to follow AML compliance regulations in the UAE?
Financial institutions, designated non-financial businesses and professions, virtual asset service providers and other entities covered by the applicable AML framework must follow the relevant requirements. The obligations depend on the business activity and regulatory classification.

### 2. Does AML compliance apply to all UAE companies?
Not every company has the same AML obligations. However, companies should assess their actual activities and determine whether they fall within a regulated category or are subject to specific reporting and compliance requirements.

### 3. What is KYC compliance in the UAE?
Know Your Customer (KYC) is the process of identifying and verifying customers, understanding their activities and assessing the risks associated with a business relationship. It forms part of customer due diligence.

### 4. What is customer due diligence?
Customer Due Diligence involves identifying and verifying customers and, where required, beneficial owners. It also includes understanding the purpose of the business relationship and applying ongoing monitoring according to risk.

### 5. What is goAML registration?
goAML is the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit's reporting system for suspicious transactions and other specified reports. Entities required to report through the system must follow the applicable registration and reporting procedures.

### 6. Do Free Zone companies need AML compliance?
Free Zone companies are not automatically exempt. Their AML obligations depend on their business activities, regulatory classification and the relevant federal, local or Free Zone supervisory requirements.

### 7. Is UBO registration the same as AML compliance?
No. UBO requirements concern identifying and maintaining information about the natural persons who ultimately own or control a company. AML compliance is broader and includes customer due diligence, risk assessment, monitoring, reporting and other controls.

### 8. What is Enhanced Due Diligence?
Enhanced Due Diligence involves additional checks for customers or business relationships that present higher risks. It may include obtaining more information about the source of funds or wealth, understanding transaction purposes and increasing monitoring.

### 9. What happens if a company fails to comply with AML regulations?
Depending on the violation, a company may face administrative penalties, regulatory action, restrictions, reputational damage or criminal consequences in serious cases. Banks may also conduct additional reviews or reconsider business relationships based on their risk assessments.

### 10. How long should AML records be maintained?
The applicable UAE AML framework and sector-specific rules establish record-retention requirements. Businesses should follow the current legal period applicable to their regulatory category and retain records in a secure, accessible and retrievable format.

### 11. Does a small business need an AML compliance officer?
The requirement depends on the business's regulatory classification and applicable rules. Certain regulated entities must appoint an appropriately qualified compliance officer. A business should confirm its specific obligations rather than assume that size alone determines the requirement.

### 12. When should a business establish AML procedures?
AML obligations should be reviewed during company formation and before the relevant regulated activities begin. This helps the business establish suitable policies, customer verification procedures and record-keeping systems from the start.

## Final Thoughts: Make AML Compliance Part of Your UAE Business Foundation
AML compliance in the UAE is an important part of maintaining a transparent and sustainable business environment.

The country's updated federal framework places strong emphasis on identifying financial crime risks, understanding customers, verifying beneficial ownership, monitoring transactions and reporting suspicious activity where required.

For business owners, the most important step is to understand which obligations apply to their actual activities and regulatory status.

A well-organised AML framework should include appropriate risk assessments, documented policies, reliable KYC procedures, transaction monitoring, employee training and accurate records.

**AML compliance should not be treated as a last-minute response to a bank request or regulatory inspection. It should be considered from the beginning of the business setup process and reviewed as the company grows.**

By building compliance into the company's foundation, businesses can strengthen operational transparency, support responsible banking relationships and prepare for sustainable growth in the UAE.
