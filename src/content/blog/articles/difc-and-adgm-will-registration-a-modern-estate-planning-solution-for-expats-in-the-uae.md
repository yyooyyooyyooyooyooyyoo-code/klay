# DIFC and ADGM Will Registration: A Modern Estate Planning Solution for Expats in the UAE

The United Arab Emirates has become an important destination for international entrepreneurs, investors, professionals and families. As more expatriates establish businesses, purchase property and build wealth in the country, protecting their assets and planning for the future have become increasingly important.

For many international residents, estate planning in the UAE is not simply about preparing a document. It involves understanding how local inheritance laws work, identifying the correct legal framework and ensuring that family members and beneficiaries can manage an estate when required.

Two important options available to eligible non-Muslim individuals are the **DIFC Wills Service** in Dubai and **ADGM Wills Registration** in Abu Dhabi. Both provide structured mechanisms for documenting inheritance wishes and planning the distribution of assets.

These frameworks offer expatriates an opportunity to organise their UAE estate planning with greater clarity. However, they have different eligibility conditions, registration processes and legal arrangements.

This guide explains how DIFC and ADGM will registration works, what assets may be covered, the benefits of registering a will and how these arrangements can form part of a wider wealth protection and succession strategy.

## Why DIFC and ADGM Wills Exist

The UAE has a large international population. Many residents own property, maintain bank accounts, invest in businesses and hold financial assets in different countries.

Without proper estate planning, the administration of these assets after someone's death may become complicated. Family members may need to deal with different legal systems, documentation requirements and inheritance procedures.

DIFC and ADGM provide specialised frameworks that address some of these concerns.

### DIFC Wills Service in Dubai

The DIFC Courts Wills Service was established to provide eligible non-Muslims with a formal mechanism to register wills and document their wishes concerning UAE assets and guardianship arrangements.

It operates through the DIFC Courts Wills and Probate Registry and is governed by the relevant Dubai legal framework and registry rules.

The service provides different will categories to address different planning requirements, including Full Wills, Property Wills, Financial Assets Wills, Business Owners Wills and Guardianship Wills.

### ADGM Wills Registration in Abu Dhabi

The Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) provides a wills notarisation service through its Courts Notary Public and Wills Office.

The framework allows eligible non-Muslim individuals to document estate distribution and guardianship arrangements through a formal notarisation process.

ADGM operates within its own legal and administrative framework. Its Notary Public and Wills Office handles notarisation rather than probate proceedings. Probate applications are handled through the relevant Abu Dhabi Judicial Department process.

Both options can be relevant to expatriates, but the correct choice depends on eligibility, asset ownership, family circumstances and the legal arrangements required.

## What Assets Can Be Covered by DIFC and ADGM Wills?

One of the main reasons expatriates consider registering a UAE will is to document how their assets should be distributed.

Depending on the chosen framework, will type and applicable requirements, estate planning may cover several asset categories.

### 1. Real Estate Property

Property is often one of the most valuable assets owned by expatriates in the UAE.

It may include:

- Residential apartments.
- Villas and houses.
- Commercial properties.
- Investment properties.
- Other eligible real estate interests.

A will can document the intended distribution of eligible property interests. However, ownership records, mortgage obligations, joint ownership and property registration requirements should also be reviewed.

### 2. Bank Accounts

Many expatriates maintain personal or business bank accounts with UAE financial institutions.

A suitable will may include eligible financial accounts, subject to the relevant registration framework and will category.

Account details should be accurate and regularly reviewed, particularly if accounts are opened, closed or transferred.

### 3. Investment Portfolios

Investments may include shares, brokerage accounts, funds and other financial interests.

Individuals with diversified portfolios should identify the legal owner of each investment and check whether the selected will category can cover it.

### 4. Company Shares and Business Interests

Business owners may hold shares in UAE companies, Free Zone entities or other corporate structures.

Succession planning should consider how those interests may be transferred and how the business will continue operating.

A will should be reviewed alongside the company's constitutional documents, shareholder agreements and any relevant transfer restrictions.

### 5. Personal and Digital Assets

Personal property may include vehicles, valuables and other eligible belongings.

Digital assets may also need consideration, depending on their legal nature and the selected registration framework. Individuals should distinguish between ownership of a digital asset and access to an account or platform.

A will should not contain passwords or confidential access credentials. Secure access and recovery arrangements should be handled separately.

## Benefits of DIFC and ADGM Will Registration

Registering a will can provide several practical benefits for eligible expatriates and their families.

### 1. Greater Clarity in Asset Distribution

A properly drafted and registered will documents the testator's intentions.

It can identify beneficiaries, specify how eligible assets should be distributed and appoint an executor to help administer the estate.

Clear instructions may reduce uncertainty and disagreements among family members.

### 2. More Structured Estate Administration

A registered will provides a formal document that can be used in the applicable legal and probate process.

This can help executors and beneficiaries understand the testator's wishes and identify the steps required to administer the estate.

However, registration does not remove every administrative requirement. Probate, creditor claims, asset verification and other legal procedures may still be necessary.

### 3. Greater Control Over Asset Allocation

An eligible individual can document how assets should be allocated among intended beneficiaries, subject to the applicable law and the validity of the will.

This is particularly important for families with children, dependants or assets held across multiple jurisdictions.

### 4. Guardianship Planning

Parents with minor children may wish to record their preferred guardianship arrangements.

The DIFC and ADGM frameworks provide options for guardianship provisions, subject to their respective eligibility and procedural requirements.

Parents should understand that naming a guardian does not necessarily guarantee an appointment in every situation. The applicable authority and legal requirements remain relevant.

### 5. Better Preparation for Cross-Border Estates

Many expatriates have assets in more than one country.

A UAE will can form part of a wider international estate plan. However, overseas assets may still require recognition, probate or separate administration under the law of the country where they are located.

Professional advice is important when coordinating wills across different jurisdictions.

## DIFC Wills Service: Understanding the Registration Framework

The DIFC Courts Wills Service is one of the established options for eligible non-Muslims who want to document their UAE inheritance and guardianship arrangements.

The service provides different will categories based on the assets and family arrangements being covered.

### Types of DIFC Wills

**Full Will**

A Full Will is designed to cover the distribution of movable and immovable property in the UAE and may include guardianship provisions for eligible minor children residing in Dubai or Ras Al Khaimah.

It may be suitable for individuals who want a broader estate-planning document.

**Property Will**

A Property Will is designed for eligible UAE real estate interests. The DIFC Courts' published service information describes this category as covering up to five UAE properties.

**Business Owners Will**

This category is designed for eligible business shareholdings in the UAE. The published service information describes a limit of up to five shareholdings.

**Financial Assets Will**

This category is designed for eligible bank and brokerage accounts registered at branches located in the UAE. The published service information describes a limit of up to ten accounts.

**Guardianship Will**

A Guardianship Will is designed to document guardianship arrangements for eligible minor children without covering the full estate distribution.

The DIFC Courts also publish information about Digital Assets Wills. Applicants should confirm the current scope and conditions of each category before selecting one.

### Who Can Register a DIFC Will?

According to the DIFC Courts' published eligibility guidance, an applicant must:

- Be non-Muslim and must never have been Muslim.
- Be at least 18 years old.
- Own assets in the UAE and/or have minor children residing with them in the UAE.

UAE residency is not necessarily required for an eligible applicant.

Guardianship arrangements have additional conditions, including requirements relating to the children's residence and the proposed guardians.

## ADGM Wills Registration: Understanding the Process

ADGM provides a separate route for eligible non-Muslim individuals who want to notarise a will in Abu Dhabi.

The process is administered through the ADGM Courts Notary Public and Wills Office.

### Who Can Use the ADGM Wills Service?

ADGM's published guidance describes its wills service as available to non-Muslim individuals aged 21 or older who own assets in the UAE.

Applicants should review the latest requirements before preparing a will, as eligibility and documentation conditions may differ from the DIFC framework.

### ADGM Will Documentation

The published ADGM guidance requires a bilingual Arabic and English will certified by a legal translator licensed by the UAE Ministry of Justice.

Depending on the application, supporting information may include:

- Testator's identification documents.
- Emirates ID, where applicable.
- Passport and residency details.
- Beneficiary information.
- Executor details.
- Information about the assets.
- Supporting ownership documents.
- Other documents requested by the Notary Public.

Applicants should use the current official templates and instructions where appropriate.

### Can ADGM Wills Cover Assets Outside Abu Dhabi?

ADGM's published guidance states that its Notary Public can notarise wills relating to assets located elsewhere in the UAE and outside the UAE.

However, a UAE will does not automatically complete the probate process for overseas property. Probate or recognition may still be required in the country where those assets are located.

### Does ADGM Provide Probate Services?

No. ADGM's Notary Public and Wills Office does not itself provide probate services.

Its published guidance states that probate applications must be submitted through the relevant Abu Dhabi Judicial Department Wills and Probate Office.

This distinction is important when comparing the registration and post-death administration processes.

## DIFC vs ADGM: Which Will Registration Option Should You Consider?

Although both frameworks support estate planning for eligible expatriates, they are not identical.

| Comparison | DIFC Wills Service | ADGM Wills Registration |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Dubai | Abu Dhabi |
| Administering body | DIFC Courts Wills and Probate Registry | ADGM Courts Notary Public and Wills Office |
| Published age requirement | 18 years or older | 21 years or older |
| Published religious eligibility | Non-Muslim and must never have been Muslim | Non-Muslim |
| UAE assets | Yes, subject to the selected will type and rules | Yes, subject to the applicable requirements |
| Overseas assets | Scope should be checked against the relevant will category and rules | Official guidance permits notarisation of wills relating to overseas assets |
| Guardianship | Specific guardianship options | Guardianship options, subject to the framework |
| Will categories | Multiple categories, including Full, Property, Financial Assets, Business Owners and Guardianship Wills | Estate disposition, guardianship, or combined arrangements |
| Document requirements | Depend on the selected will category and registry rules | Bilingual Arabic-English will certified by a UAE Ministry of Justice-licensed legal translator |
| Probate | DIFC Courts Wills and Probate Registry handles probate matters under its framework | ADGM Notary Public and Wills Office does not provide probate; the relevant ADJD process handles probate applications |

There is no universal winner. The appropriate route depends on your eligibility, assets, family requirements and the legal framework that best fits your estate.

## What About Abu Dhabi Judicial Department Will Registration?

DIFC and ADGM are not the only routes available to individuals considering UAE will registration.

The Abu Dhabi Judicial Department (ADJD) also provides civil wills services.

ADJD may be relevant to individuals who prefer to use the Abu Dhabi judicial registration process or whose circumstances require consideration of that framework.

Its published civil wills information includes options for non-UAE citizens. The department's eligibility and documentation rules should be reviewed directly before applying.

The three routes should not be treated as interchangeable. Their eligibility conditions, registration procedures and post-registration administration arrangements differ.

## Combining Wills with Wealth Structures

For individuals with significant assets, a registered will may be only one part of a wider wealth-planning strategy.

Families with multiple businesses, investment portfolios, property holdings and international interests may need additional structures to organise ownership and long-term management.

### 1. Trust Formation in the UAE

A trust can be used to hold and manage assets for beneficiaries under defined terms.

Depending on the applicable legal framework, trusts may support:

- Long-term asset management.
- Structured distributions to beneficiaries.
- Family wealth succession.
- Management of assets for younger family members.
- Continuity of ownership and administration.

Trust formation requires careful legal and tax planning. The trust's terms, governing law, trustees, beneficiaries and reporting obligations should be reviewed before implementation.

### 2. Foundation Formation in the UAE

A foundation is a separate legal structure that may be used to hold and manage assets for specified purposes or beneficiaries.

Depending on the jurisdiction and structure, foundations may support:

- Family wealth management.
- Succession planning.
- Investment ownership.
- Holding company arrangements.
- Long-term family governance.

A foundation does not automatically protect every asset from every liability. Its effectiveness depends on the applicable law, establishment, governance and compliance.

### 3. Family Office UAE

A family office can help families coordinate wealth management, investment oversight, reporting and succession planning.

It may be relevant for families with complex financial interests or assets across multiple jurisdictions.

### 4. Holding Company Structure UAE

A holding company may be used to own shares in subsidiaries, business interests or investments.

It can support clearer ownership arrangements and help organise group structures.

For business owners, a holding company may also form part of a wider succession plan. However, company shares, shareholder agreements, wills and other ownership arrangements must be coordinated carefully.

## Asset Protection Through Estate Planning

Effective asset protection involves more than registering a will.

A complete estate plan should consider how assets are owned, managed, transferred and administered.

For example, a property may be held jointly, a business may be owned through a company, and investments may be held through a brokerage account.

Each arrangement can have different legal consequences.

A practical asset protection and succession review should consider:

- Personal and joint property ownership.
- Company shares and shareholder rights.
- Bank accounts and investment holdings.
- Existing loans and financial obligations.
- Overseas assets.
- Beneficiary arrangements.
- Executor appointments.
- Guardianship requirements.
- Trust or foundation structures, where suitable.

A will should reflect the actual legal ownership of assets. It cannot automatically transfer property that the testator does not own or override every contractual restriction.

## Estate Planning for Business Owners in the UAE

Entrepreneurs should consider what would happen to their businesses if they passed away or became unable to manage their affairs.

Without proper planning, a business may face uncertainty over share ownership, management authority, decision-making and financial responsibilities.

### Company Ownership and Share Transfers

Review the company's legal structure and identify who owns each shareholding.

Check whether the Memorandum and Articles of Association or shareholder agreement contains transfer restrictions, pre-emption rights or succession provisions.

### Management Continuity

Identify who can manage the business if its founder or principal shareholder is unavailable.

This may involve appointing directors, documenting operational responsibilities and preparing a business continuity plan.

### Shareholder Agreements

Shareholder agreements should be reviewed to understand what happens if a shareholder dies or becomes incapacitated.

Depending on the business, it may be appropriate to consider buyout provisions, share-transfer mechanisms or alternative management arrangements.

### Coordination With the Will

A will should be consistent with the company's ownership documents and other legal arrangements.

A registered will does not automatically override company-law requirements or contractual restrictions. Business succession should therefore be reviewed as a connected legal and commercial plan.

## Why Early Estate Planning Matters

Many individuals delay estate planning because they believe it is only necessary later in life or after accumulating substantial wealth.

In reality, estate planning can be relevant as soon as a person acquires property, starts a business, has dependants or develops financial interests in more than one country.

Starting early provides time to:

- Identify and organise assets.
- Confirm legal ownership.
- Select beneficiaries.
- Appoint suitable executors.
- Consider guardianship arrangements.
- Review company ownership.
- Coordinate international estate documents.
- Update arrangements when circumstances change.

Waiting until a serious event occurs may leave family members with additional uncertainty and administrative difficulties.

## Common Estate Planning Mistakes Expats Should Avoid

### 1. Assuming a Home-Country Will Is Automatically Sufficient

An overseas will may form part of an international estate plan, but its recognition and administration in the UAE should not be assumed.

### 2. Ignoring Asset Ownership

A will should reflect who legally owns each asset. Joint ownership and company-held assets require particular attention.

### 3. Choosing a Will Without Checking Eligibility

DIFC and ADGM have different published age and eligibility requirements. Confirm the current conditions before selecting a route.

### 4. Forgetting Guardianship Planning

Parents with minor children should consider guardianship separately from the distribution of assets.

### 5. Ignoring Business Succession

Company shares, shareholder agreements and management arrangements should be reviewed alongside the will.

### 6. Failing to Coordinate International Assets

Overseas assets may be subject to local succession and probate requirements. A UAE will does not automatically replace the need for local procedures.

### 7. Not Updating the Will

Marriage, divorce, the birth of a child, acquiring property, selling a business or relocating to another country may require an estate-plan review.

Review DIFC, ADGM and ADJD eligibility, will categories, documentation and administration processes.

**Step 7: Complete registration**

Follow the selected authority's current submission, translation, appointment and registration requirements.

**Step 8: Review the plan regularly**

Update your arrangements when your family, assets, business ownership or international circumstances change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What is DIFC Wills Service?

The DIFC Courts Wills Service provides eligible non-Muslim individuals with a formal route to register wills covering eligible UAE assets and, where applicable, guardianship arrangements for minor children.

### 2. What is ADGM Wills Registration?

ADGM Wills Registration is a notarisation service provided through the ADGM Courts Notary Public and Wills Office. It allows eligible non-Muslim individuals to document estate distribution and guardianship arrangements.

### 3. What is the minimum age for registering a DIFC Will?

The DIFC Courts' published guidance states that an applicant must be at least 18 years old and meet its other eligibility requirements.

### 4. What is the minimum age for ADGM Will registration?

ADGM's published wills guidance describes eligibility for non-Muslim individuals aged 21 or older who own assets in the UAE.

### 5. Can I register a UAE will if I am not a UAE resident?

DIFC's published guidance states that UAE residency is not required for an eligible applicant. ADGM's eligibility and identification requirements should be checked against its latest official instructions.

### 6. Can a UAE will cover property in Dubai and Abu Dhabi?

The scope depends on the selected registration route and will category. ADGM's published guidance allows notarisation of wills relating to assets elsewhere in the UAE. The DIFC will category and applicable rules should be reviewed to confirm the assets covered.

### 7. Can a UAE will include overseas assets?

ADGM's published guidance states that its Notary Public can notarise wills relating to assets outside the UAE. However, probate or recognition may still be required in the jurisdiction where overseas assets are located.

### 8. Does registering a will guarantee faster inheritance distribution?

Registration can provide a formal record of the testator's wishes and may help organise estate administration. However, probate, verification, creditor claims and other legal requirements may still affect the process. No fixed completion time should be assumed.

### 9. Can I include company shares in my UAE will?

Company shares may be included where permitted by the selected will framework and category. The company's constitutional documents, shareholder agreements and applicable transfer restrictions should also be reviewed.

### 10. Can I appoint guardians for my children through a UAE will?

The DIFC and ADGM frameworks provide guardianship options subject to their respective requirements. The proposed arrangements should be reviewed by a qualified UAE lawyer, as registration does not guarantee an appointment in every circumstance.

### 11. Is a trust or foundation necessary if I already have a registered will?

Not necessarily. A will may be sufficient for some individuals. Trusts and foundations may be considered for more complex ownership, wealth management or succession requirements. Their suitability depends on the individual's circumstances.

### 12. Should I register both a DIFC and an ADGM will?

Multiple wills can create complications if their scope, governing law or revocation provisions conflict. Obtain legal advice before registering more than one will to ensure that the documents work together.

### 13. Can I update my UAE will after registration?

The applicable process may allow a will to be amended, replaced or revoked. The correct procedure depends on the registration authority and the terms of the existing document. Seek advice before making changes.

### 14. Does ADGM handle probate after a person's death?

The ADGM Notary Public and Wills Office does not provide probate services. Its published guidance directs probate applications to the relevant Abu Dhabi Judicial Department Wills and Probate Office.
