# Inheritance and Succession Planning in the UAE: Why Expats Must Plan Early

Inheritance and succession planning in the UAE has become an essential part of financial planning for expatriates, investors and business owners who live in or hold assets in the country. As the UAE continues to develop as a global financial and commercial hub, more international residents are investing in property, establishing companies and building wealth across multiple jurisdictions.

For many expatriates, an important question eventually arises: what happens to my UAE assets and business interests if something happens to me?

Without appropriate estate planning, families may face uncertainty about asset ownership, inheritance procedures, company shares and the administration of financial accounts. Even when an individual has prepared a will in their home country, its recognition and implementation in the UAE should not be assumed.

This is why inheritance and succession planning in the UAE should begin early. A properly prepared estate plan can document your wishes, identify beneficiaries, appoint executors and help organise the transfer of eligible assets under the applicable legal framework.

For business owners and high-net-worth individuals, planning may also involve trusts, foundations, holding companies and family governance arrangements.

This guide explains why succession planning matters, how UAE will registration works, which structures may support long-term wealth management and what expatriates should consider before making important decisions.

## Why Inheritance Planning Is Important for Expats in the UAE

The UAE has a large international population. Many expatriates own assets in the country while maintaining financial, family or business connections overseas.

These assets may include:

- Residential and commercial properties.
- Personal and corporate bank accounts.
- Investment portfolios and shares.
- UAE company ownership interests.
- Vehicles and personal belongings.
- Business assets and intellectual property.
- Overseas property and financial investments.

When an individual passes away, these assets may need to be identified, verified and administered under the relevant legal procedures.

If there is no suitable estate plan, family members may face additional uncertainty over who inherits the assets, who can manage the estate and which legal process applies.

### What Does Succession Planning Help You Decide?

A well-organised succession plan allows individuals to consider important questions before they become urgent.

**Who should inherit your assets?**

You can document your intended beneficiaries and how eligible assets should be distributed, subject to the applicable law.

**Who should manage your estate?**

An executor can be appointed to carry out the instructions in your will through the relevant probate process.

**Who should care for your minor children?**

Parents can consider guardianship arrangements through a framework that permits them, subject to the applicable requirements.

**Who should manage your business?**

Business owners can prepare for continuity by reviewing company ownership, shareholder arrangements and management responsibilities.

**How should international assets be handled?**

If you own property or investments in multiple countries, your UAE estate plan should be coordinated with the relevant overseas legal arrangements.

## Understanding Inheritance Law in the UAE

Inheritance matters in the UAE can involve different legal frameworks depending on the individual's circumstances, nationality, religion, asset location and applicable legislation.

For non-Muslim expatriates, the UAE provides legal routes for documenting inheritance wishes and registering wills.

However, there is no single registration process that automatically applies to every resident and every asset.

The federal personal status framework and applicable civil transactions rules must be considered alongside relevant local laws and specialised registration frameworks. Abu Dhabi also has a separate civil family law framework for certain matters involving foreigners.

For example, Abu Dhabi Law No. 14 of 2021, as amended, provides a civil framework for wills and inheritance for persons governed by that law. It recognises the right of eligible testators to bequeath their assets in the country to beneficiaries of their choice through a will. (Read the Abu Dhabi inheritance law).

The important point is that expatriates should not rely on assumptions about how inheritance will work. Their circumstances and the location and ownership of their assets should be reviewed by a qualified UAE succession lawyer.

## Will Registration in Dubai and Across the UAE

One of the most important steps in inheritance planning is considering whether to register a suitable will.

A will is a legal document that records how an individual wants eligible assets to be distributed after death. Depending on the registration framework, it may also include executor appointments and guardianship provisions.

For expatriates, a registered will can provide a formal record of their instructions within the relevant legal framework.

The main registration routes commonly considered include:

### 1. DIFC Wills Service

The DIFC Courts Wills Service provides a registration framework for eligible non-Muslims who want to document their wishes concerning UAE assets and, where permitted, guardianship arrangements for minor children.

The service operates through the DIFC Courts Wills and Probate Registry.

Available will categories include:

- Full Will.
- Property Will.
- Financial Assets Will.
- Business Owners Will.
- Guardianship Will.
- Digital Assets Will.

Each category has its own scope and conditions. For example, the Property Will and Financial Assets Will have specific asset limits under the published service categories.

The DIFC Courts' published eligibility guidance states that applicants must be non-Muslim, must never have been Muslim, must be at least 18 years old and must own UAE assets and/or have minor children residing with them in Dubai or Ras Al Khaimah.

UAE residency is not required for an otherwise eligible applicant. (DIFC Courts Wills Service).

### 2. ADGM Wills Registration

The Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) provides a wills notarisation service through its Courts Notary Public and Wills Office.

The service is designed for eligible non-Muslim individuals who want to document estate distribution and guardianship arrangements.

ADGM's published guidance describes eligibility for non-Muslim individuals aged 21 or older who own assets in the UAE.

Its document requirements include a bilingual Arabic-English will certified by a UAE Ministry of Justice-licensed legal translator.

An important distinction is that ADGM's Notary Public and Wills Office does not itself provide probate services. Probate applications must be submitted through the relevant Abu Dhabi Judicial Department Wills and Probate Office.

### 3. Abu Dhabi Judicial Department Will Registration

The Abu Dhabi Judicial Department (ADJD) provides a civil wills registration route.

Its published information states that non-UAE citizens may register a will regardless of religion under the applicable civil wills framework.

The process generally involves submitting an application, providing supporting documents, completing the review and appointment process, and paying the relevant fees.

ADJD publishes will forms in Arabic and English. Its FAQ also states that funds and property located outside the UAE may be included in a will. However, overseas assets may still require recognition or probate procedures in the relevant foreign jurisdiction. (ADJD Civil Wills FAQ).

### Which Will Registration Route Should You Choose?

The correct option depends on several factors:

- Your eligibility and personal circumstances.
- The location of your property and financial assets.
- Whether you need guardianship provisions.
- Whether you own company shares.
- The type of will you need.
- The applicable probate process.
- Document, translation and registration requirements.

A registration route should be selected after reviewing the whole estate rather than choosing based only on where one property is located.

## DIFC and ADGM Wills for Non-Muslims

The DIFC Wills Service and ADGM Wills Registration framework are particularly relevant to eligible non-Muslim expatriates who want a formal process for documenting inheritance wishes.

They provide structured options for individuals who own property, maintain financial accounts or operate businesses in the UAE.

### Assets That May Be Included

Depending on the selected framework and will category, an estate plan may address:

**Real estate property**

Apartments, villas, commercial properties and other eligible property interests.

**Bank accounts**

Personal and eligible financial accounts maintained with UAE banks.

**Company shares**

Shareholdings in UAE companies and business ownership interests, subject to the applicable will category and company documents.

**Investment portfolios**

Eligible investment and brokerage accounts.

**Personal assets**

Other eligible personal property and belongings.

**Digital assets**

Certain digital assets may be addressed under available will categories and the relevant legal requirements.

The scope of the will should always be confirmed before registration. Not every asset type is covered in the same way by every framework.

## Why a Will Alone May Not Be Enough

A registered will is an important part of estate planning, but it does not necessarily solve every issue involving complex ownership, business continuity or multi-generational wealth.

For individuals with substantial assets, additional structures may be useful.

These may include:

- Trust formation in the UAE.
- Foundation formation in the UAE.
- Holding company structures.
- Family office arrangements.
- Shareholder and governance agreements.

The suitability of each structure depends on the individual's circumstances, legal requirements and tax position.

## Trust Formation in the UAE for Long-Term Wealth Planning

A trust is a legal arrangement in which assets are held and managed by a trustee for beneficiaries or specified purposes under defined terms.

Depending on the governing law and structure, trusts may help families organise wealth over an extended period.

### Potential Uses of Trust Structures

**Long-term asset management**

A trust can establish rules for managing selected assets rather than distributing everything immediately.

**Beneficiary planning**

The trust deed may define how and when beneficiaries receive distributions, subject to the governing law.

**Family wealth succession**

Trusts may support arrangements for transferring wealth across generations.

**Management for younger beneficiaries**

A trust may provide a framework for managing assets for children or other beneficiaries who are not ready to manage them independently.

**Business and investment ownership**

Depending on the structure, a trust may hold shares or other investments and provide a defined governance framework.

Trusts require careful legal and tax planning. Their effectiveness depends on the trustee, governing law, trust deed, asset transfers and ongoing compliance.

## Foundation Formation in the UAE

A foundation is a separate legal structure that can hold and manage assets for specified purposes or beneficiaries.

Foundations are available in certain UAE jurisdictions and may be considered by families and investors who want a structured approach to long-term ownership and succession.

### How Foundations May Support Estate Planning

Depending on the applicable legal framework, a foundation may help with:

- Holding family investments.
- Organising ownership of business interests.
- Supporting multi-generational wealth planning.
- Establishing governance and decision-making arrangements.
- Coordinating family wealth management.
- Holding shares in a company or group structure.

A foundation is not automatically an asset protection solution for every situation. Its establishment, governance, legal purpose and compliance must be properly designed.

## Holding Company Structure UAE: Supporting Business Succession

Business owners often hold several investments or operating companies.

A holding company may provide a central ownership structure through which shares in subsidiaries and other business interests are held.

This can make ownership arrangements easier to organise and may support succession planning.

### Benefits of a Holding Company Structure

**Centralised ownership**

A holding company can own shares in multiple subsidiaries or business entities.

**Clearer ownership arrangements**

It may help organise how business interests are held and managed.

**Business expansion**

A group structure can provide a framework for adding subsidiaries, investments or new business activities.

**Succession planning**

Ownership of the holding company can form part of a wider inheritance strategy.

**Governance**

A holding structure may support clearer decision-making and group-level oversight.

However, transferring shares into a holding company does not automatically solve inheritance issues. Company documents, shareholder agreements, applicable corporate law and the will must be reviewed together.

## Family Office UAE: Managing Wealth Across Generations

Families with significant assets may need a coordinated approach to managing investments, reporting, governance and succession.

A family office can help organise these responsibilities.

Depending on the family's requirements, a family office may coordinate:

- Investment management and oversight.
- Financial reporting.
- Family governance.
- Succession planning.
- Wealth preservation strategies.
- Professional adviser relationships.
- Family education and long-term planning.

Not every family requires a dedicated family office. The structure should be proportionate to the complexity of the family's assets and responsibilities.

## Asset Protection in the UAE: A Structured Approach

Asset protection should not be treated as a single legal document or company registration.

It involves understanding how assets are owned, managed, insured, transferred and exposed to potential liabilities.

A practical asset protection review may include:

### 1. Ownership Review

Identify whether assets are owned personally, jointly or through a company.

### 2. Legal Documentation

Keep property records, company documents, investment statements and relevant contracts organised.

### 3. Estate Planning

Prepare an appropriate will and review beneficiaries, executors and guardianship arrangements.

### 4. Corporate Structuring

Consider whether a holding company or other ownership structure is suitable for business and investment interests.

### 5. Trusts and Foundations

Where appropriate, explore whether a trust or foundation can support long-term management and succession.

### 6. Compliance and Reporting

Maintain accurate financial records and meet relevant legal, tax and regulatory obligations.

No structure can guarantee protection against every creditor claim, legal dispute or liability. Asset protection arrangements should be established for genuine purposes and reviewed by qualified legal and tax advisers.

## Succession Planning for Business Owners in the UAE

For entrepreneurs, inheritance planning and business succession are closely connected.

A business may depend on its founder for strategic decisions, banking relationships, client management and operational oversight.

If the owner dies or becomes unable to manage the company, the business may face uncertainty unless suitable arrangements are in place.

### Review Company Ownership

Identify who legally owns the shares and whether any transfer restrictions apply.

### Review Shareholder Agreements

Check whether existing agreements address death, incapacity, share transfers, buyouts and disputes.

### Plan Management Continuity

Identify who can manage the business and make decisions if the founder is unavailable.

### Coordinate the Will and Corporate Documents

A will should be reviewed alongside the company's constitutional documents and shareholder arrangements.

### Prepare a Business Continuity Plan

Document key responsibilities, important contracts, financial processes and operational information.

Business succession should be treated as a commercial continuity issue as well as an inheritance matter.

## Common Inheritance Planning Mistakes Expats Should Avoid

Many families delay estate planning or assume that their existing arrangements are sufficient.

The following mistakes can create unnecessary uncertainty.

### 1. Assuming a Home-Country Will Is Automatically Sufficient

An overseas will may form part of an international estate plan, but its recognition and administration in the UAE should not be assumed.

### 2. Waiting Until Assets Become Substantial

Estate planning can be relevant as soon as you own property, start a business or have dependants.

### 3. Ignoring Asset Ownership

A will should reflect who legally owns each asset. Joint ownership and company-held property require particular attention.

### 4. Forgetting Guardianship Planning

Parents with minor children should consider guardianship separately from asset distribution.

### 5. Ignoring Business Succession

Company shares, shareholder agreements and management arrangements should be reviewed alongside the will.

### 6. Choosing a Structure Without Understanding Its Legal Effect

Trusts, foundations and holding companies have different purposes and legal consequences. They should not be selected simply because they are popular wealth-planning structures.

### 7. Not Coordinating International Documents

If you have assets in several countries, your wills and estate-planning documents should be reviewed together to reduce inconsistencies and unintended revocation clauses.

### 8. Failing to Update the Estate Plan

Marriage, divorce, the birth of a child, acquiring property, selling a business or relocating to another country may require a review.

## How to Start Your Inheritance and Succession Planning in the UAE

A structured process makes estate planning easier to understand and manage.

**Step 1: List Your Assets**

Prepare an inventory of your UAE and overseas property, bank accounts, investments, business interests and other significant assets.

Identify whether each asset is held personally, jointly or through a company or other structure.

**Step 3: Identify Your Beneficiaries**

Decide who should receive your assets and consider alternative beneficiaries.

**Step 4: Appoint an Executor**

Choose someone suitable to carry out the instructions in your will, subject to the relevant legal process.

**Step 5: Consider Guardianship**

If you have minor children, discuss appropriate guardianship arrangements with a qualified UAE lawyer.

**Step 6: Review Business Ownership**

Examine company documents, shareholder agreements and management arrangements.

**Step 7: Compare Will Registration Options**

Review DIFC, ADGM and ADJD based on your eligibility, assets and family requirements.

**Step 8: Obtain Professional Advice**

Work with qualified legal and tax professionals before establishing trusts, foundations or holding structures.

**Step 9: Review Regularly**

Update the estate plan when your family, assets, business or international circumstances change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What is inheritance planning in the UAE?

Inheritance planning involves organising how your assets should be managed and transferred after your death. It may include a registered will, beneficiary instructions, executor appointments, guardianship arrangements and wider ownership structures.

### 2. Why is succession planning important for expats in the UAE?

Expats may own assets in the UAE and several other countries. Succession planning helps document their intentions, clarify ownership and prepare family members and business partners for estate administration.

### 3. Is will registration mandatory for every expatriate?

The legal requirements depend on the individual's circumstances and applicable law. However, expatriates with UAE assets or family and business interests should consider obtaining legal advice and preparing an appropriate estate plan.

### 4. What is the difference between a will and succession planning?

A will is a legal document that records instructions about the distribution of an estate. Succession planning is broader and may include wills, company ownership arrangements, management continuity, trusts, foundations and family governance.

### 5. Can expatriates register a will in Dubai?

Eligible non-Muslim individuals can register a will through the DIFC Courts Wills Service, subject to its eligibility conditions. Other UAE registration routes, including ADGM and ADJD, have their own requirements.

### 6. Can a UAE will cover overseas assets?

Some UAE frameworks allow overseas assets to be included. However, a UAE will does not automatically complete the probate process in another country. Local recognition and succession procedures may still apply.

### 7. Can business shares be included in a UAE will?

Business interests may be included where permitted by the selected registration framework and will category. Company constitutional documents, shareholder agreements and transfer restrictions should also be reviewed.

### 8. What is the role of a trust in inheritance planning?

A trust may hold and manage assets for beneficiaries under defined terms. Depending on the governing law, it may support long-term wealth management, structured distributions and multi-generational planning.

### 9. What is the difference between a trust and a foundation?

A trust is a legal relationship involving a trustee holding assets for beneficiaries or specified purposes. A foundation is a separate legal entity established under its applicable law. Their governance, ownership and legal treatment differ.

### 10. Is a holding company useful for succession planning?

A holding company may organise ownership of subsidiaries and investments. It can form part of a succession strategy, but company documents, shareholder agreements and wills must be coordinated.

### 11. When should expats start inheritance planning?

It is sensible to review estate planning when you acquire UAE property, start a business, have dependants or establish significant financial interests in the country. Plans should be updated as circumstances change.

### 12. Can I change my registered will later?

The applicable authority may provide procedures to amend, replace or revoke a registered will. The correct process depends on the registration framework and the existing document.

## Final Thoughts: Planning Today Protects Tomorrow

Inheritance and succession planning in the UAE is an important consideration for expatriates, investors and business owners who want to protect their families and organise their assets.

A registered will can provide a formal record of inheritance wishes, while trusts, foundations and holding company structures may support more complex wealth and business arrangements.

The right approach depends on your personal circumstances, asset ownership, family requirements and long-term objectives.

Waiting until an unexpected event occurs can make estate administration more difficult for the people who depend on you. Starting early gives you time to organise documents, review ownership, choose suitable beneficiaries and obtain appropriate professional advice.

**A clear succession plan is not just about transferring wealth. It is about protecting family interests, maintaining business continuity and creating a more organised future for the next generation.**
