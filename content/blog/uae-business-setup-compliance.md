# How Incorporation, Banking, AML and ESR Work Together in the UAE
UAE business setup compliance is not limited to company incorporation alone. Establishing a company is only the first step in building a business that can operate, manage finances and meet its regulatory responsibilities in the UAE.

Many entrepreneurs treat incorporation, corporate banking, Anti-Money Laundering (AML) compliance and Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as separate processes. In reality, these areas are connected through the company's business activities, ownership structure, jurisdiction and operational model.

The decisions made during company formation can influence the documents required by banks, the regulatory obligations applicable to the business and the level of compliance preparation needed before operations begin.

For example, selecting an unsuitable business activity may create licensing complications. If the actual business model is not clearly explained, a bank may request additional information during its customer due diligence process. If the company conducts activities covered by AML legislation, it may also have specific compliance responsibilities.

ESR requires a different consideration. The UAE cancelled ESR notification and reporting requirements for financial years ending after 31 December 2022, following Cabinet Decision No. 98 of 2024. However, historical obligations for earlier financial years may still need attention.

Understanding how these areas work together helps entrepreneurs make better incorporation decisions and avoid preventable operational and compliance problems.

This guide explains how UAE company incorporation, banking, AML and ESR connect, why jurisdiction matters and how businesses can approach compliance from the beginning.

## Why UAE Business Setup Compliance Requires an Integrated Approach
A company may receive its trade licence without having completed every step needed to begin operating smoothly.

After incorporation, business owners may still need to arrange corporate banking, organise accounting records, understand applicable tax obligations, review AML exposure and obtain any additional approvals required for their activities.

These processes are connected because they rely on consistent information about the company.

Important details include:

- The company's licensed business activities.
- Its legal structure and jurisdiction.
- Shareholders and Ultimate Beneficial Owners (UBOs).
- Directors and authorised signatories.
- Actual business operations.
- Customer and supplier locations.
- Expected revenue and transaction flows.
- Source of funds.
- Applicable regulatory permissions.
- Financial and compliance documentation.
If the information provided during incorporation differs from the company's actual operations or banking application, additional clarification may be necessary.

An integrated approach helps ensure that the company is structured around its genuine commercial objectives rather than treating each approval as an unrelated task.

## 1. Company Incorporation: The Foundation of Business Compliance
Company incorporation establishes the legal structure through which a business operates.

The first decisions include selecting the jurisdiction, legal form, business activities, ownership arrangement and licensing authority.

These decisions influence several areas of the company's future operations.

### Business Activity Selection
The business activity determines what the company is authorised to do under its licence.

For example, a consultancy, general trading company, technology provider and financial services business may have different licensing and regulatory requirements.

Activity selection should reflect the company's actual and planned operations.

An incorrect or overly broad activity selection can create problems when:

- Applying for a corporate bank account.
- Preparing customer and supplier contracts.
- Obtaining additional regulatory approvals.
- Reviewing AML obligations.
- Explaining the company's revenue model.
- Expanding into new services or markets.
A business should confirm its licensing requirements before incorporation, especially when activities involve regulated services.

### Jurisdiction Selection
The choice between Mainland, Free Zone and offshore structures should be based on the company's commercial purpose, operational requirements and applicable legal framework.

Each structure has different licensing arrangements, operational considerations and documentation requirements.

### Ownership and Management Structure
The company should maintain clear records of shareholders, ownership percentages, directors, managers and beneficial owners.

This information becomes important during banking onboarding, regulatory reviews and ongoing corporate administration.

A transparent ownership structure also makes it easier to explain who controls the business and who is authorised to act on its behalf.

## 2. Corporate Banking: How Incorporation Decisions Affect Bank Account Applications
Opening a UAE corporate bank account is a separate approval process from company registration.

A trade licence confirms that the company has been licensed for specified activities. It does not automatically guarantee that a bank will approve an account.

Banks conduct their own customer due diligence and risk assessments before establishing a business banking relationship.

They may review:

- Trade licence and incorporation documents.
- Shareholders and UBO information.
- Directors and authorised signatories.
- Nature of the company's activities.
- Source of funds.
- Expected annual turnover.
- Customer and supplier locations.
- Expected transaction volumes.
- Countries and currencies involved.
- Contracts, invoices and business plans.

### Why Business Activity Alignment Matters
Suppose a company is incorporated with a consultancy activity but expects to receive substantial payments for product trading.

The bank may ask why the expected transactions do not appear to match the declared business model.

This does not automatically mean the application will be rejected. However, the company may need to clarify its activities, provide supporting documents or review whether its licensing arrangements are appropriate.

### Why Ownership Transparency Matters
Banks need to identify the individuals who ultimately own or control the company.

If a company has several shareholders or corporate ownership layers, the bank may request additional documents to understand the ownership chain.

Clear shareholder registers, ownership charts and UBO information can help make this review more straightforward.

### Source of Funds and Transaction Profile
A bank may ask where the company's initial capital comes from and how it expects to earn revenue.

The business should be prepared to explain:

- Initial funding.
- Expected monthly or annual turnover.
- Main products or services.
- Customer and supplier locations.
- Incoming and outgoing payments.
- Expected transaction values.
- International payment requirements.
The information should be realistic and supported by available evidence.

**Key point:** Incorporation and banking are connected, but bank account approval remains an independent decision made by the selected bank under its own requirements and risk policies.

## 3. AML Compliance: Why Business Activities Matter
Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing requirements form an important part of the UAE's financial and regulatory framework.

The applicable AML obligations depend on the nature of the business, its regulatory classification and the activities it conducts.

Financial institutions, relevant Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs), and Virtual Asset Service Providers may have specific preventive and reporting obligations under the applicable legislation.

Not every ordinary company has identical AML obligations. However, businesses should understand whether their activities bring them within a regulated category.

### What Is AML Compliance?
AML compliance refers to the systems and procedures used to prevent, identify and report potential money laundering, terrorist financing and other prohibited financial activity.

Depending on the business category and applicable requirements, obligations may include:

- Customer identification and verification.
- Customer due diligence.
- Beneficial ownership checks.
- Risk assessments.
- Enhanced due diligence where required.
- Transaction monitoring.
- Record keeping.
- Suspicious transaction reporting.
- Internal compliance procedures.
- Staff awareness and training.

### How Incorporation Affects AML Exposure
The company's licensed activities are an important starting point when assessing whether it may fall within a regulated AML category.

For example, certain real estate activities, company service activities, precious metals and stones trading, accounting or auditing services and other designated professional activities may fall within the DNFBP framework, depending on the precise activity and applicable definitions.

A business that selects activities without understanding their regulatory implications may discover additional compliance responsibilities after incorporation.

### The Importance of Business Model Clarity
A company should be able to explain:

- What services or products it provides.
- Who its customers are.
- How it receives payments.
- Which countries it operates in.
- Whether it handles funds for customers.
- Whether it conducts activities requiring additional approval.
This information can be important for both banking due diligence and regulatory compliance.

### UAE AML Legal Framework
The UAE's AML framework was updated through Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 and Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025, which sets out its Executive Regulations.

Businesses should assess their obligations against the legislation and guidance applicable to their actual activities and regulatory category.

Official legislative information is available through the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit and the UAE Central Bank.

## 4. ESR: Understanding Its Historical Role in UAE Company Compliance
Economic Substance Regulations were introduced to ensure that businesses carrying out specified Relevant Activities demonstrated an appropriate level of economic presence in the UAE.

The original framework covered nine Relevant Activities, including:

1. Banking business.
2. Insurance business.
3. Investment fund management business.
4. Lease-finance business.
5. Headquarters business.
6. Shipping business.
7. Holding company business.
8. Intellectual property business.
9. Distribution and service centre business.
Under the original framework, qualifying businesses were required to assess their activities and, where applicable, demonstrate economic substance through management, employees, physical assets and operating expenditure in the UAE.

### Important Update: ESR Reporting Was Cancelled for Later Financial Years
The UAE Ministry of Finance announced on 14 October 2024 that, following Cabinet Decision No. 98 of 2024, ESR notification and reporting requirements were cancelled for financial years ending after 31 December 2022.

This means:

- New ESR Notifications are not required for financial years ending after 31 December 2022.
- Economic Substance Reports are not required for those financial years.
- Historical obligations for earlier financial years remain relevant.
- Businesses must respond to applicable information or amendment requests concerning earlier periods.
- Penalties imposed for earlier periods remain subject to the applicable rules.
Companies should not treat ESR as a routine annual filing requirement for current financial years.

### Why Historical ESR Still Matters
A company that operated during the original ESR reporting period may need to review whether it had Relevant Activities and whether it completed the applicable submissions.

Historical compliance records may include:

- ESR Notifications.
- Economic Substance Reports.
- Financial statements.
- Employee and payroll information.
- Office and premises records.
- Board meeting documentation.
- Records of relevant income.
- Correspondence with regulatory authorities.
The company's financial year-end and historical activities are important when determining whether an earlier reporting obligation applied.

### ESR and Current Compliance Are Separate
The cancellation of ESR reporting does not remove other regulatory obligations.

Companies may still need to meet requirements relating to Corporate Tax, VAT where applicable, AML, UBO information, trade licence renewals, accounting and sector-specific regulations.

Businesses should assess each regulatory framework separately.

## 5. How Mainland, Free Zone and Offshore Structures Affect Compliance
Jurisdiction selection influences licensing, operational scope, documentation and the company's regulatory environment.

A proper Mainland, Free Zone and offshore comparison should take place before incorporation.

### Mainland Companies
Mainland companies are licensed by the relevant authority in the emirate.

Depending on the activity and applicable rules, they may be suitable for businesses that want to operate in the UAE domestic market or require a particular commercial or professional licence.

Compliance considerations may include:

- Correct business activity selection.
- Relevant licensing approvals.
- Corporate ownership records.
- Banking documentation.
- Applicable AML classification.
- Tax and accounting obligations.

### Free Zone Companies
Free Zone companies operate under the rules of their respective Free Zone authorities.

They may be suitable for businesses seeking a particular industry ecosystem, licensing arrangement or operational structure.

Compliance considerations may include:

- Free Zone licence conditions.
- Permitted business activities.
- Office or facility requirements.
- Shareholder and UBO information.
- Banking documentation.
- Applicable AML requirements.
- Tax and accounting responsibilities.
Free Zone incorporation does not automatically exempt a company from federal regulatory requirements.

### Offshore Structures
Offshore entities may be suitable for specific holding or international structuring purposes, depending on the jurisdiction and legal framework.

Their suitability should be evaluated carefully.

Banks may request detailed information about ownership, business purpose, source of funds and international transaction flows.

An offshore structure should not be selected solely on the assumption that it will simplify banking or remove compliance responsibilities.

### Choosing the Right Jurisdiction
The best structure depends on:

- Actual business activities.
- Target customers and markets.
- Operational presence.
- Ownership arrangements.
- Banking requirements.
- Regulatory approvals.
- Tax considerations.
- Long-term expansion plans.
There is no universal structure that is suitable for every business.

## 6. How Incorporation, Banking, AML and ESR Connect
The four areas can be understood as different stages of a connected compliance process.

### Stage 1: Incorporation Defines the Business
The company selects its legal structure, jurisdiction, business activities, shareholders and management arrangements.

This creates the foundation for the company's regulatory profile.

### Stage 2: Banking Reviews the Business Profile
The selected bank reviews the company's identity, ownership, activities, funding and expected transactions.

The information should match the company's incorporation records and actual business model.

### Stage 3: AML Determines Applicable Preventive Obligations
The company assesses whether its activities fall within an AML-regulated category and identifies the obligations that apply.

Where relevant, it must establish appropriate controls and documentation.

### Stage 4: ESR Historical Exposure Is Reviewed
For companies that operated during the original ESR reporting period, historical activities and financial years should be reviewed to identify any outstanding obligations.

Current financial years ending after 31 December 2022 do not require ESR Notifications or Reports under the 2024 amendment.

### Stage 5: Ongoing Compliance Supports Operations
After the company begins operating, it should maintain accurate financial records, update corporate information and comply with applicable tax, AML, licensing and other regulatory requirements.

These responsibilities should be reviewed as the business expands or changes its activities.

## 7. Common Compliance Mistakes UAE Businesses Should Avoid

### 1. Choosing Business Activities Without Understanding Their Implications
The licence should accurately reflect the company's intended operations and any applicable regulatory requirements.

### 2. Treating Bank Account Approval as Automatic
Company registration does not guarantee that a bank will accept the business as a customer.

### 3. Ignoring Ownership Transparency
Incomplete shareholder or UBO information can create problems during banking and regulatory reviews.

### 4. Assuming Every Company Has the Same AML Obligations
AML requirements depend on the nature of the business and its regulatory classification.

### 5. Treating ESR as a Current Annual Filing
ESR reporting requirements were cancelled for financial years ending after 31 December 2022. Businesses should focus on applicable historical obligations instead of following outdated annual filing instructions.

### 6. Confusing ESR With Corporate Tax
ESR and Corporate Tax are separate regulatory frameworks. The end of ESR reporting does not remove applicable Corporate Tax responsibilities.

### 7. Preparing Documents Only When Requested
Maintaining accurate company, ownership, financial and operational records from the beginning can make future reviews easier.

### 8. Selecting a Jurisdiction Based Only on Cost
A low-cost incorporation package may not be appropriate if it does not support the company's activities, operational requirements or long-term plans.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Why are incorporation and AML compliance connected in the UAE?
Business activities selected during incorporation can influence whether a company falls within a regulated AML category. The actual services provided and business model must be reviewed to determine applicable obligations.

### 2. How does ESR affect company structuring?
Under the original ESR framework, certain Relevant Activities required businesses to demonstrate economic substance in the UAE. However, ESR Notifications and Reports are no longer required for financial years ending after 31 December 2022. Historical obligations for earlier periods may still need review.

### 3. Does jurisdiction impact banking approval?
Yes, jurisdiction can influence company documentation, permitted activities and how a bank assesses the business. However, Mainland or Free Zone status does not guarantee bank account approval.

### 4. Do all UAE companies need an AML compliance programme?
No. AML obligations depend on the company's activities and regulatory classification. Financial institutions, DNFBPs and other regulated entities have specific obligations under applicable legislation. Businesses should assess their actual activities rather than assume that every company has identical requirements.

### 5. Can a Free Zone company open a UAE corporate bank account?
Yes. Free Zone companies can apply for UAE corporate bank accounts, subject to the selected bank's eligibility criteria, due diligence and internal risk policies.

### 6. Is ESR filing still mandatory in the UAE?
ESR Notifications and Economic Substance Reports are no longer required for financial years ending after 31 December 2022. Historical reporting responsibilities and applicable penalties for earlier periods remain relevant.

### 7. What is the difference between ESR and AML compliance?
ESR was designed to assess whether certain businesses had adequate economic substance in the UAE. AML requirements are designed to prevent money laundering, terrorist financing and related financial crimes. They are separate regulatory frameworks.

### 8. What documents should a UAE company prepare for banking?
Banks may request a trade licence, incorporation documents, shareholder and UBO details, identification documents, business profile, source of funds evidence and expected transaction information. The exact checklist varies by bank.

### 9. Does company incorporation guarantee banking approval?
No. Incorporation and bank account opening are separate processes. Banks independently assess applicants according to their compliance requirements and risk appetite.

### 10. When should a business review its compliance obligations?
Ideally, before incorporation and whenever its business activities, ownership, jurisdiction or transaction profile changes. Regular reviews help businesses identify new requirements and maintain accurate records.

## Final Thoughts: Build Your UAE Business With Compliance in Mind
Company incorporation, corporate banking, AML and ESR should not be viewed as unrelated administrative tasks.

Each area contributes to a different part of the company's legal, financial and regulatory position.

The incorporation structure defines the business. Banking reviews its ownership, funding and commercial profile. AML requirements apply according to the company's activities and regulatory classification. ESR remains relevant when reviewing historical financial periods covered by the original framework.

A structured approach helps entrepreneurs make informed decisions, prepare consistent documentation and understand their responsibilities before problems arise.

For businesses entering the UAE market, the goal should be more than obtaining a trade licence. It should be to establish a company that is appropriately structured, prepared for banking and aligned with the regulations that apply to its operations.
